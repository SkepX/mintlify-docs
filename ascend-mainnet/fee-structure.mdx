Ascend has a simple fee system. **Makers pay 0% at lower tiers and earn rebates at the top. Takers pay 0.040% to start, and pay less as their share of platform volume grows.**

Your fee level depends on how much of the platform's total volume you traded over the last 30 days. You do not need to hold any token or fill out any form. Everyone starts at the base level and moves up on their own.

## Fee Tiers

| Tier       | 30D Volume Share | Taker Fee | Maker Fee |
| ---------- | ---------------: | --------: | --------: |
| **Tier 0** |           < 0.5% |    0.040% |    0.000% |
| **Tier 1** |           ≥ 0.5% |    0.036% |    0.000% |
| **Tier 2** |             ≥ 2% |    0.032% |    0.000% |
| **Tier 3** |             ≥ 5% |    0.028% |   −0.001% |
| **Tier 4** |            ≥ 10% |    0.024% |   −0.002% |
| **Tier 5** |            ≥ 20% |    0.020% |   −0.004% |

A negative maker fee is a **rebate**: the protocol pays you to provide liquidity. From Tier 3 onward, makers earn on every fill.

***

## How Your Tier Is Calculated

<Steps>
  <Step title="We measure your share of platform volume">
    Your tier is based on the share of Ascend's total 30-day trading volume that came from your account. Both maker and taker trades count. Orders you place but never fill do not count. Orders you cancel do not count.
  </Step>

  <Step title="We look at the last 30 days">
    The window is the last 30 days of trading, not a calendar month. Trades older than 30 days drop off.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Your tier updates once a day">
    Your tier is set once per day at **00:00 UTC** and stays the same all day. If you cross into a new tier in the middle of the day, your fee changes at the next update, not right away.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Everyone starts at Tier 0">
    Every account starts at the base rate. No application. No form. No minimum deposit. Just trade, and your tier goes up.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Maker vs Taker

Every trade has two sides. One person was waiting on the book. The other person came in and took their offer. That is what decides which fee you pay.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Maker (0% or rebate)">
    You were the one waiting. You put an order on the book, and someone else came and matched it.

    * You placed a **limit order** at a price that did not cross the current spread
    * Your order sat on the book
    * Someone else's order matched yours

    **Example (BTC-USD):** The best ask is \$50,000. You place a limit order to LONG 1,000 USD at \$49,000. Your order waits on the bid side. Twenty minutes later, someone sends a SHORT market order and fills you at \$49,000.

    **Fee: 0.00 USD at Tiers 0–2. At Tier 3+, you earn a small rebate on every fill.**
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Taker (tiered)">
    You were the one taking. Your order matched right away against something already on the book.

    * You placed a **market order**, or
    * You placed a **limit order at a price that crossed the spread**
    * Either way, your order filled right away instead of waiting

    **Example (ADA-USD):** The best ask is \$0.50. You are on Tier 0 and place a market order to LONG 1,000 USD. It fills at \$0.50.

    ```
    Fee = 1,000 × 0.0004 = 0.40 USD
    ```
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Note>
  Whether you are a maker or taker is decided when your order fills, not by the type of order you picked. A limit order that crosses the spread still counts as a taker. If you want to be sure you get the maker rate, place limit orders behind the spread and let the market come to you.
</Note>

## How Fees Are Charged

Fees are based on the size of the trade that filled:

```
Trading Fee = Trade Size × Fee Rate
```

This is the same trade size used for [funding](/market-mechanics/funding-price-alignment). A few things to keep in mind:

* **Fees are based on trade size, not on your margin.** A 500 USD position at 10x leverage is a 5,000 USD trade, and the fee is worked out on 5,000.
* **One fill, one fee.** If your order fills across three price levels, that is still one fee, based on the total size.
* **Placing and canceling orders is free.** You only pay when your order actually fills.
* **Opening and closing are two separate trades.** A full round trip means two fees.
* **Your tier is locked in for the day.** The fee you pay is your tier from the last daily update.
* **A negative maker fee pays you.** If your tier has a rebate, that amount is credited to your account on every maker fill.

## Examples

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Taker fill at Tier 0" icon="1">
    ```
    Trade size = 10,000 USD
    Taker fee  = 0.040%

    Fee = 10,000 × 0.0004 = 4.00 USD
    ```
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Same fill at Tier 4" icon="2">
    ```
    Trade size = 10,000 USD
    Taker fee  = 0.024%

    Fee = 10,000 × 0.00024 = 2.40 USD
    ```

    Same trade, same size, but 40% cheaper. The only reason is your share of platform volume.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Maker fill at Tier 0" icon="3">
    ```
    Trade size = 10,000 USD
    Maker fee  = 0.000%

    Fee = 10,000 × 0 = 0.00 USD
    ```

    Makers pay nothing at Tiers 0–2, so this number stays flat until Tier 3.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Maker rebate at Tier 5" icon="4">
    ```
    Trade size = 10,000 USD
    Maker fee  = −0.004%

    Rebate = 10,000 × 0.00004 = 0.40 USD credited to you
    ```

    At Tier 5, you get paid 0.40 USD every time a 10,000 USD maker order fills.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="One order, both roles" icon="5">
    On BTC-USD, you place a limit order to LONG 1,000 USD at \$50,000. The best ask is \$50,000, but only 400 USD is available at that price.

    * **400 USD fills right away** against the ask → taker
    * **600 USD waits on the book** at \$50,000 and fills later → maker

    ```
    Taker part = 400 × 0.0004 = 0.16 USD
    Maker part = 600 × 0      = 0.00 USD

    Total fee = 0.16 USD
    ```
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Leverage" icon="6">
    Fees are based on trade size, so using leverage makes your fee bigger compared to the margin you put up.

    ```
    Margin     = 500 USD
    Leverage   = 10x
    Trade size = 5,000 USD

    Taker fee = 5,000 × 0.0004 = 2.00 USD   (0.4% of your margin)
    ```
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Round-Trip Cost

Your total cost to open and close depends on how each side fills. Here is a 10,000 USD position at **Tier 0**:

| Entry | Exit  | Total Fee | Effective Rate |
| ----- | ----- | --------: | -------------: |
| Taker | Taker | 8.00 USD |         0.080% |
| Taker | Maker | 4.00 USD |         0.040% |
| Maker | Taker | 4.00 USD |         0.040% |
| Maker | Maker | 0.00 USD |         0.000% |

If you are patient and use limit orders on both sides, you pay nothing to trade on Ascend at Tier 0, and you actually earn from Tier 3 onward.

## Other Costs (Not Trading Fees)

Trading fees are not the only thing that affects your price. These other costs are not fees paid to the protocol:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The spread" icon="arrows-left-right">
    The gap between the best bid and the best ask is the cost of trading right away. If the spread is 1% and you market in and market out, you lose 1% to the book. That is 25 times more than the Tier 0 taker fee.

    Busy markets have small spreads. Quiet markets have big ones. See [CLOB & Matching](/market-mechanics/clob-matching).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Slippage" icon="chart-line">
    Big orders eat through the book. They fill at more than one price, and each price is a bit worse than the last. So your average fill ends up worse than the top price you saw.

    This is about how much liquidity is there, not a fee. Limit orders let you avoid it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Funding" icon="clock">
    Funding is a payment between LONG and SHORT traders. It keeps the Mark Price close to the Index Price. The protocol takes no cut of it.

    Depending on which side you are on, funding is either something you pay or something you earn. See [Funding & Price Alignment](/market-mechanics/funding-price-alignment).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Where the Fees Go

Taker fees are how the protocol earns money. Maker rebates are paid out of that same pool, and **100% of what is left goes to $ASCEND stakers**.

Staking is easy. Your tokens stay in your Cardano wallet. Nothing is locked. You can leave any time. Every taker trade on the platform sends money back to the people who hold the network.

## Why Makers Get Paid

Perpetual markets need deep books to work. A market with a tight spread is easy to trade. A market with a wide spread is not, no matter how interesting the asset is.

Charging makers would punish the exact behavior these markets need most. Ascend goes further: at the top tiers, we pay makers to post liquidity.

* **Tighter spreads.** Market makers can quote closer to fair value when posting is free, or better than free.
* **Real depth at every level.** Rebates reward posting size, not chasing fills.
* **Lower total cost for takers.** The spread you cross is usually bigger than the fee you pay.
* **A level playing field.** A new account posts on the same terms as the biggest firm, and can climb into rebate tiers on trading alone.

Takers fund the protocol. Makers build the book. Rebates make sure the best makers stay building.

## Key Points

* Makers pay **0%** at Tiers 0–2 and earn **rebates up to −0.004%** at Tier 5.
* Takers start at **0.040%** and pay as low as **0.020%** at Tier 5.
* Tiers are based on your share of Ascend's last 30 days of volume and update once a day at 00:00 UTC.
* You are a maker if your order waited on the book, and a taker if your order took liquidity right away.
* Fees are based on trade size, not on your margin, so leverage makes them bigger.
* Placing and canceling orders is free. You only pay when your order fills.
* Spread, slippage, and funding are separate from trading fees.
* 100% of net taker fees go to $ASCEND stakers.
